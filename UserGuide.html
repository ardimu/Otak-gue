Purpose. This guide explains every menu action and the controls inside each dialog. Use it as a step-by-step reference for daily accounting work. The text is written in professional, plain English so it can be translated without changing the workflow.

1. How to use this guide

Open it from Menu > Accounting > Setup > User Guide / SOP.

  • Use the left contents list to jump to a section, or type a word in the search box to show only matching sections.
  • Each section follows the same pattern: where to open it, what each control does, the steps, and the expected result.
  • Words in bold are interface labels. Words in code are sheet names, field names, or system values.
  • The guide never changes data. It is a reference only.
Web app. The same menus and content are available in the Apps Script Web App. Open the deployed URL, sign in with Google, and use the top menu bar with dropdowns to move between pages. To restrict access, set WEB_ALLOWED_EMAILS in Script Properties to a comma-separated list of email addresses.
Reading convention A draft is not company data yet. A posted journal is final and affects reports. This distinction is the key to using the system safely.

2. Operating modes

The system has two modes. The current mode is shown in the banner of the New Journal Entry form and on reports.

ModeMeaningJournal ID behaviorImportant limits
PRACTICE Training data. It is not company data. The next ID follows the last visible Journal ID in the Journal sheet. Deleting the last journal allows that number to be used again. Reset Practice Data clears practice journals, import logs, staged imports, and the practice sequence.
LIVE Company data is active. The stored sequence continues even if rows are removed, so IDs are never reused. Mode is one-way. After Go Live, you cannot return to PRACTICE.
Go Live deletes all practice data before switching to LIVE. Confirm the company name and complete the checklist first. This action cannot be undone.

3. Golden rules before posting

  1. Choose the correct date. The date determines the accounting period. A date on or before the lock date cannot be posted.
  2. Use active accounts only. Inactive accounts in COA are not selectable.
  3. Balance the journal. Total debit must equal total credit, with at least two lines, before posting.
  4. Assign a Customer or Supplier for AR and AP. Without it, the journal still posts to the General Ledger but will not appear in the Customer or Supplier ledger.
  5. Save first, post later when unsure. A draft can be edited or deleted. A posted journal cannot be edited; correct it with a reversing entry.
  6. Review warnings before confirming. Post Anyway accepts the risk. Review Again cancels the action and keeps the data unchanged.

Open from Transactions & Input > New Journal Entry.

4.2 Entry modes

  • MANUAL. Enter each debit and credit line yourself. Use this for simple or unusual entries.
  • ASSISTED. Choose a transaction template and complete the business fields. Choose Generate journal lines to build the debits and credits automatically, including VAT, withholding tax, and cost of goods sold where applicable.

4.3 Opening balances

For an Opening Entry, use Load all balance sheet accounts to add asset, liability, and equity accounts. Use Balance with Opening Balance Equity to insert the difference automatically so the journal balances.

Customer or Supplier input is only available for Accounts Receivable (AR), Accounts Payable (AP), and other customer- or supplier-related accounts. It is disabled for Cash on Hand, Bank, and other accounts.

4.4 Working with journal lines

  • Add line adds an empty row. Fill the account, line description, debit, and credit.
  • The remove button at the end of a row deletes that line.
  • Enter amounts as numbers. Formatting is applied when the field loses focus.
  • The balance panel shows total debit, total credit, and the difference. A green balanced state is required before posting.
  • Taxes are optional and, when enabled, are included in the generated lines rather than added later.

4.5 Saving or posting

  1. Complete the header and lines.
  2. Choose Save draft to store the journal without affecting reports, or Post journal to make it final.
  3. Read the confirmation. Confirm with the primary button or cancel with the secondary button.
  4. The result shows the Journal ID, status, created by, posted by when applicable, and total.
Editing a draft Open Draft Management, choose Edit, change the fields, then save or post again. The Journal ID stays the same. Posted journals cannot be edited.

4.6 Complete guide to journal types

Select the Journal type that matches the economic event. The journal type controls the account scope available in the form and the relevance of the party field. For every type, only active accounts in COA may be used and total debit must equal total credit before posting.

Everyday journal types

Journal TypePurpose in general accountingTypical debit and creditExampleCustomer/SupplierAccount scope applied by the system
General Journal Records transactions not covered by a special journal and non-routine events. Debit an asset or expense account; credit a liability, equity, or revenue account as appropriate. Paying office rent in cash: debit Rent Expense, credit Cash on Hand. Relevant only when a line uses Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable. All active accounts may be selected; no special restriction.
Sales Journal Records credit sales of goods or services. Debit Accounts Receivable 1201; credit Sales Revenue 4101 and Output VAT. A credit sales invoice of Rp500.000 to a customer. Customer is required because Accounts Receivable identifies the party. All active accounts may be selected; assisted mode generates lines from the sales template.
Purchase Journal Records credit purchases of goods or services. Debit Inventory or an expense and Input VAT; credit Accounts Payable 2101. Credit purchase of office supplies amounting to Rp500.000. Supplier is required because Accounts Payable identifies the party. All active accounts may be selected; assisted mode generates lines from the purchase template.
Cash Receipt Journal Records all cash and bank receipts. Debit Cash 1101 or Bank 1102; credit Accounts Receivable, Revenue, or the related liability. A customer settles an outstanding receivable by bank transfer. Relevant only on the Accounts Receivable line; never entered on the Cash or Bank line. All active accounts may be selected.
Cash Disbursement Journal Records all cash and bank payments. Debit Accounts Payable or an expense; credit Cash 1101 or Bank 1102. Paying a supplier or settling an accrued electricity expense. Relevant only on an Accounts Payable or supplier-related line; never on the Cash or Bank line. All active accounts may be selected.

Specialized journal types

Journal TypePurpose in general accountingTypical debit and creditExampleCustomer/SupplierAccount scope applied by the system
Payroll Journal Records salaries, wages, and employee deductions. Debit Salary Expense and related expenses; credit Salaries Payable, withholding tax payable, and Cash or Bank 1101/1102. Monthly payroll of Rp25.000.000. Not relevant; employees are not Customers or Suppliers in this journal. All active accounts may be selected.
Asset Acquisition Records the acquisition of fixed or other assets. Debit the asset account, for example Equipment; credit Cash, Bank, or Accounts Payable. Cash purchase of computers for operations. Relevant when the acquisition is on credit through Accounts Payable; never entered on the Cash or Bank line. All active accounts may be selected.
Asset Disposal Records the sale, write-off, or disposal of an asset. Debit Cash or Bank and Accumulated Depreciation; credit the asset account and Gain or Loss on Disposal. Selling a vehicle that has been partly depreciated. Relevant when the transaction creates Accounts Receivable or involves a Supplier; never entered on the Cash or Bank line. All active accounts may be selected.
Capital & Equity Records owner transactions and changes in equity other than operating results. Debit Cash or Bank; credit Owner's Capital or Retained Earnings. For distributions: debit Retained Earnings, credit Owner's Withdrawals or Dividends. An owner contributes capital to the company bank account. Generally not relevant; an owner is not a Customer or Supplier unless the related account requires it. All active accounts may be selected.
Loan & Financing Records receipts and repayments of loans or financing facilities. On drawdown: debit Cash or Bank; credit Loan Payable. On repayment: debit Loan Payable and Interest Expense; credit Cash or Bank. Bank loan drawdown of Rp50.000.000. Never entered on the Cash or Bank line; relevant only when the financing institution is recorded as a Supplier on the related payable account. All active accounts may be selected.
Fund Transfer Records movements of funds between cash and bank accounts without changing total cash. Debit Bank 1102 or Cash 1101; credit the originating Cash or Bank account. Depositing cash on hand into the company bank account. Not relevant; a party name is never entered on Cash on Hand or Bank. All active accounts may be selected.
Inventory Adjustment Records physical count differences, valuation changes, and inventory corrections. Debit Inventory; credit Cost of Goods Sold, or the reverse for a write-down. Adjusting closing inventory based on a stocktake. Not relevant. All active accounts may be selected.
Production Transfer Records the transfer of costs between production stages and into finished goods. Debit Work in Process or Finished Goods Inventory; credit Raw Materials, Work in Process, or Labor Expense. Transferring completed production costs to Finished Goods Inventory. Not relevant. All active accounts may be selected.

Period-end journal types

Journal TypePurpose in general accountingTypical debit and creditExampleCustomer/SupplierAccount scope applied by the system
Adjusting Entry Adjusts revenue and expense so they are recognized in the correct period. Debit Expense; credit Accrued Expenses, or debit Accrued Revenue; credit Revenue. Accruing electricity incurred in November but paid in December. Relevant only when the adjustment involves Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable; never on Cash or Bank. Only adjustment-related accounts, such as accruals, deferred revenue, prepaid expenses, depreciation or amortization, and the related revenue or expense accounts.
Reversing Entry Cancels an accrual adjustment at the start of the following period. The reverse of the earlier accrual: debit Accrued Expenses; credit Electricity Expense, or debit Revenue; credit Accrued Revenue. Reversing the November electricity accrual at the start of December. Relevant only when the reversed account is a receivable or payable; never on Cash or Bank. Only accrual-related accounts, namely accrued expenses, receivables, revenue, and the revenue or expense accounts used in the earlier accrual.
Closing Entry Closes temporary accounts and transfers operating results to equity at period end. Debit Revenue; credit Income Summary. Debit Income Summary; credit Expenses and Cost of Goods Sold. Debit Income Summary; credit Retained Earnings for profit, or the reverse for a loss. Year-end closing after all adjusting entries have been posted. Not relevant. Only Income Summary, Retained Earnings, owner withdrawals or dividends, Revenue, contra revenue, Cost of Goods Sold, and Expense accounts.
Opening Entry Establishes opening balances for the period from the prior period's balance sheet. Debit Asset accounts; credit Liability and Equity accounts. Loading all balance sheet accounts when the system is first used. Relevant only on Accounts Receivable and Accounts Payable; disabled for Cash on Hand, Bank, and other accounts. Only Asset, Liability, and Equity accounts.
Correction Entry Corrects a posting error without changing the original source document. Debit and credit the accounts being corrected, in the direction required by the correction. Correcting a cost wrongly posted to Rent Expense so it is recorded as Electricity Expense. Relevant only when the corrected account relates to Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable. Broad because this journal is generic: all active accounts may be selected.
Reclassification Entry Moves balances between appropriate accounts without changing profit or total equity. Debit the destination account; credit the source account, for example debit Expense; credit Cost of Goods Sold. Reclassifying a balance from a less appropriate account to the correct one. Generally not relevant, except when Accounts Receivable or Accounts Payable is reclassified. Broad because this journal is generic: all active accounts may be selected.
Accrual example: November electricity Electricity amounting to Rp500.000 was incurred and recognized in November as debit Electricity Expense 6201 / credit Accrued Expenses 2110 (or the equivalent accrued-expense account). In December, settlement is recorded through the Cash Disbursement Journal as debit Accrued Expenses 2110 / credit Cash or Bank 1101/1102. A reversing entry is optional; if it is used, it reverses the November accrual at the start of December. The December payment is not a reversal. A party name is entered only on receivables, payables, or other customer- and supplier-related accounts, and never on Cash on Hand or Bank.

5. Bank and Petty Cash Import

Open from Transactions & Input > Bank & Petty Cash Import.

5.1 Prepare the source sheet

  1. Open the Bank Import sheet.
  2. Paste statement rows starting at cell A1. Use Paste special, Values only.
  3. Keep the header row in the row selected by the active profile in Bank Profiles.
  4. Add or keep the Customer / Supplier column. This sheet is the source of the party name. Bank Profiles only stores profile layout and matching rules.

5.2 Read and review

  1. Select the matching profile. A bank profile reads statements; a petty cash profile reads vouchers.
  2. Choose Read the Bank Import sheet.
  3. Review each preview row. Change the account if the suggested account is wrong.
  4. Use Apply for a bulk account change and Select or clear all to toggle row selection.

A name that is not in Master Data > Parties shows the warning Customer / Supplier not found in Master Data. You may continue, but that journal will not appear in the party ledger. Add the party first if ledger tracking is required.

5.3 Import

  1. Choose Import.
  2. Select whether to create drafts or post immediately.
  3. Read the result. It shows the batch ID, imported lines, skipped lines, elapsed time, and where to review drafts.

Recent batches are listed at the bottom. A draft batch can be posted with Post all or removed with Delete. Duplicate rows are detected with an import key and are skipped.

6. Draft Management

Open from Transactions & Input > Draft Management.

This is the review and approval queue. Nothing here affects reports until it is posted.

6.1 Controls

ControlUseResult
Search boxFilter by Journal ID, description, or reference.Shows only matching drafts.
RefreshReload the latest draft list.Updates the table from the Journal sheet.
EditOpen the journal form for that draft.Allows changes; the Journal ID is preserved.
PostPost one draft.Moves that journal into the General Ledger.
DeleteRemove one draft.Deletes all rows of that draft after confirmation.
Post AllPost every visible draft or the filtered list.Processes in batches and reports posted and failed counts.

6.2 Missing Customer or Supplier warning

If any selected draft has no Customer or Supplier, the system asks what to do:

  • Post Anyway posts the journals. They appear in the General Ledger but not in the Customer or Supplier ledger.
  • Review Again cancels posting and keeps every draft unchanged.

6.3 Partial success

If some drafts fail, successful journals are posted and failed drafts remain in the list. The result dialog shows counts and a View Failed action so you can correct only the failed items. A failure reason is stored with each failed draft.

7. Period-End Tools and reversals

Open from Closing & Periodic > Period-End Tools.

Important Every tool in this dialog creates drafts only. Review them in Draft Management, then post them when approved.

7.1 Month

The selected month drives depreciation, reversing entries, and year-end closing. Set it first.

7.2 Depreciation

  1. Choose Preview to see straight-line depreciation from the Fixed Assets sheet.
  2. Review the proposed lines. Months already booked are skipped and missed months are caught up.
  3. Choose Create draft journal when the preview is correct.

7.3 Reversing entries

A reversing entry cancels a prior adjusting entry in the next period. It is normally used for accruals so the expense or income is not counted twice.

  1. Choose List adjusting entries to show posted adjusting entries for the selected month.
  2. Select one or more entries. Entries already reversed are disabled.
  3. Set the Reversal date, usually the first day of the next month.
  4. Choose Create reversing drafts.
  5. To reverse any other posted journal, type its Journal ID in Or reverse any posted journal by its ID and choose Create reversing draft.
  6. Review the new drafts in Draft Management and post them.
RuleExplanation
Only posted journalsDrafts cannot be reversed. Edit or delete a draft instead.
No reversal of a reversalA journal already of type Reversing Entry cannot be reversed again.
No duplicate reversalA journal that already has a reversal is disabled or rejected.
Opposite linesDebits become credits and credits become debits with the same total.
Linked IDThe new journal stores the original Journal ID in Linked Journal ID for audit trail.

7.4 Year-end closing

  1. Select the last month of the financial year.
  2. Choose Preview to see revenue and expense accounts closing to Income Summary, then to Retained Earnings, plus owner withdrawals and dividends.
  3. Choose Create closing drafts when the preview is correct.
  4. Review and post the drafts in order.

7.5 Lock date

  • Set Locked through and choose Save to stop posting on or before that date.
  • Choose Clear the lock to remove it.
  • Drafts dated in a locked period can still be edited, but posting is blocked.

9. System and Setup

Open from System > Setup in the accounting sidebar.

9.1 Company Profile & Settings

Use the four tabs:

  • Profile. Company identity and logo. Choose Save logo, Remove logo, or Save profile.
  • Accounting. Business type, fiscal year start, and accounting policy settings. Choose Save accounting settings.
  • Accounts. Default accounts used by generated journals. Choose Add default account to create an additional mapping, then edit its name and account or use Remove to delete it. Choose Save default accounts to apply changes. Every built-in and additional account must exist and be active in the COA; the Go Live checklist also checks them.
  • System. Mode, Go Live checklist, period and bank controls. Choose Save system settings.

Reset Practice Data clears practice-only data. Go Live opens a confirmation that requires the exact company name and a completed checklist.

9.2 Update

Opens the two maintenance tools: Install / Update System and Check COA & Settings.

9.3 Install / Update System

Creates or updates the required sheets, headers, and default settings. It is safe to rerun after an upgrade. It does not delete posted journals. It also adds the Phone column to the Parties sheet.

9.4 Check COA & Settings

Runs diagnostics on the chart of accounts and required settings, including additional default account mappings. Fix every reported issue before generating reports or moving to LIVE.

9.5 Language

Switches the interface and dialogs between English and Bahasa. There is no reset button.

Opens this guide. A translated copy keeps the same section order and workflow steps.

9.7 Other support

Open from Other support > Parties (Customer or Supplier).

Use this popup to maintain the party list without editing the sheet directly. The list shows ID, Name, Type, Tax ID, Phone, Terms, and Status. Search matches name, ID, phone, or tax ID; Type filters Customer, Supplier, or Both.

  • Add party. Enter Name, Type, Phone number, Tax ID (NPWP/NIK), Payment terms (days), Address, and Active. The phone number is also available in the journal form's Add new dialog.
  • Edit. Opens the same form for an existing party. Keep Active checked when the party can still be selected in journals.
  • Delete. An unused party is removed. A party already used in a journal is set to Inactive instead, so historical reports remain correct. Inactive parties stay in the list but cannot be selected for new journals.

10. Standard operating procedures

10.1 Record a manual journal

  1. Open New Journal Entry.
  2. Set date, reference, description, journal type, and Customer or Supplier when relevant.
  3. Choose MANUAL.
  4. Add at least two lines with debit and credit amounts.
  5. Check that the balance panel is balanced.
  6. Choose Save draft for review or Post journal for final recording.

10.2 Record a sales or purchase with tax

  1. Open New Journal Entry and choose ASSISTED.
  2. Select the sales or purchase template and enter the base account, amount, payment method, and party.
  3. Enable VAT or withholding tax only when applicable, then complete the tax document details.
  4. Choose Generate journal lines.
  5. Review the generated lines, then save or post.

10.3 Import a bank statement

  1. Paste values into the Bank Import sheet starting at A1.
  2. Open Bank & Petty Cash Import.
  3. Select the profile and choose Read the Bank Import sheet.
  4. Correct accounts and Customer or Supplier names in the preview.
  5. Choose Import as Draft, then review in Draft Management before posting.

10.4 Reverse a posted journal

  1. Open Period-End Tools.
  2. Set the reversal date, usually the first day of the next period.
  3. Type the posted Journal ID and choose Create reversing draft.
  4. Open Draft Management and post the reversing draft.
  5. Record the correction separately if the original entry was wrong.

10.5 Close a month safely

  1. Post all ordinary journals for the month.
  2. Run depreciation and create its drafts.
  3. List adjusting entries and create reversing drafts for the next period.
  4. Review and post all generated drafts.
  5. Generate reports for the month, then set the lock date after approval.

10.6 Maintain the party list

  1. Open Other support > Parties (Customer or Supplier).
  2. Search by name, ID, phone, or tax ID, or filter by Type.
  3. Choose Add party to create a customer or supplier, including the phone number.
  4. Choose Edit to correct contact details, terms, type, or status.
  5. Choose Delete to remove an unused party, or accept that a referenced party becomes Inactive.

12. Glossary

TermDefinition
Adjusting EntryA period-end journal that records income or expense earned or incurred but not yet entered.
ARAccounts Receivable. Amounts owed by customers.
APAccounts Payable. Amounts owed to suppliers.
BatchA group of journals created by one bank import action.
COAChart of Accounts. The list of accounts used for classification.
Closing EntryA journal that moves temporary income and expense accounts into equity at year end.
Control AccountThe General Ledger account that summarizes a subsidiary ledger, such as Trade Receivables.
Customer or SupplierThe party linked to an AR or AP journal. Also called a party.
DraftA saved journal that is not yet counted in reports.
Due DateThe date payment is expected and the basis for aging reports.
Import KeyA system value that identifies an imported row and prevents duplicates.
Income SummaryA temporary account used during year-end closing before profit moves to Retained Earnings.
Journal IDThe unique journal number, for example JV-2026-00336.
Journal TypeThe classification of a journal, such as Sales Journal or Adjusting Entry.
LedgerThe record of transactions. The General Ledger covers all accounts; the Customer or Supplier ledger covers one party.
Lock DateThe last date up to which posting is blocked.
Master DataReference lists such as Parties, COA, and Fixed Assets.
Opening Balance EquityA temporary equity account used to balance opening balances.
Opening EntryA journal that records starting balances.
Party IDThe internal identifier linked to a Customer or Supplier name.
PeriodThe accounting month derived from the journal date.
PostedA final journal that affects the General Ledger and reports.
Reversing EntryA journal with opposite lines that cancels a posted journal in the next period.
Retained EarningsThe equity account that holds accumulated profit or loss.
Suspense AccountA temporary account used when an import line cannot yet be matched to a specific account.
VATValue Added Tax. Output VAT is charged on sales; input VAT is paid on purchases.
WHTWithholding Tax. Tax deducted at payment and recorded as a payable or prepaid tax.
